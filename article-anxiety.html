Managing Test Anxiety

3 Min Read

A little bit of stress before a big exam is normal—it can even sharpen your focus and help you perform better. However, when that stress turns into severe anxiety, it can cause your mind to go blank and physically make you feel unwell.

"Anxiety disorders are among the most common mental health conditions in adolescents. Learning emotional regulation techniques early is essential for long-term health." – World Health Organization (WHO)

Physical Symptoms of Anxiety

When you have test anxiety, your body initiates a "fight or flight" response, which can cause:

  • Racing heartbeat
  • Sweaty palms
  • Stomach aches or nausea
  • Shortness of breath

Techniques to Ground Yourself

If you feel a panic response rising during a test, you can use these discreet, evidence-based methods to regulate your nervous system:

Box Breathing

This technique forces your heart rate to slow down. Inhale for 4 seconds, hold your breath for 4 seconds, exhale for 4 seconds, and hold empty for 4 seconds. Repeat this 4 times.

The 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding Method

Shift your focus from internal panic to external reality by silently identifying: 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell, and 1 thing you can taste.

Long-term Preparation

Anxiety is often fueled by feeling unprepared or sleep-deprived. Avoid staying up all night studying—the WHO emphasizes that adequate sleep is required to move information from short-term to long-term memory.
