Setting Healthy Boundaries

5 Min Read

A healthy relationship—whether with a friend, family member, or romantic partner—is built on mutual respect. Establishing boundaries is how you communicate what you are comfortable with and how you expect to be treated.

"Interpersonal violence, including bullying and intimate partner violence, is a significant public health issue affecting adolescents globally." – World Health Organization (WHO)

What Are Boundaries?

Boundaries are the limits you set to protect your physical and emotional well-being. They can involve your time, your emotions, your body, and your digital life.

Signs of Unhealthy Boundaries (Red Flags):

  • Someone constantly checking your phone or demanding your passwords.
  • Feeling pressured or coerced into physical intimacy.
  • Being isolated from your friends or family by a partner.
  • Being told that jealousy or controlling behavior is a "sign of love."

Understanding Consent

Consent is central to any physical or emotional boundary. According to global health guidelines, consent must be: Freely given, Reversible, Informed, Enthusiastic, and Specific (FRIES). If someone does not respect your "No," they are not respecting your boundaries or your autonomy.

How to Communicate Your Boundaries

Fostering respectful relationships requires clear communication.

  • Be clear and direct: Use "I" statements, such as "I feel uncomfortable when..." or "I need space to..."
  • You don't need to over-explain: "No" is a complete sentence. You do not owe anyone a long justification for your comfort levels.
  • Consistency is key: Stick to your boundaries, even if the other person pushes back initially.

Where to Get Help

If you feel unsafe, or if a partner is exhibiting abusive or violent behavior, it is crucial to reach out to a trusted adult, a health professional, or local resources like the Isange One Stop Centers in Rwanda.
