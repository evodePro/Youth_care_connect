STI Prevention Basics

6 Min Read

Understanding Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) is a fundamental right. Having accurate, non-judgmental information empowers you to make informed decisions about your body and your future.

"More than 1 million sexually transmitted infections (STIs) are acquired every day worldwide, the majority of which are asymptomatic." – World Health Organization (WHO)

Understanding STIs

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) are infections passed from one person to another through sexual contact. It is important to know that anyone who is sexually active can get an STI, and many STIs do not show immediate symptoms.

The Principle of Dual Protection

The WHO heavily promotes "Dual Protection"—which means using methods that protect against both unintended pregnancies and STIs simultaneously.

  • Condoms: When used correctly and consistently, male and female condoms are highly effective at preventing the transmission of STIs, including HIV.
  • Contraceptives: Pills, implants, or injections help prevent pregnancy, but they do not protect against STIs. This is why pairing them with condoms is crucial.

Vaccines & Prevention

Some STIs can be prevented through vaccines. The WHO strongly recommends the HPV (Human Papillomavirus) vaccine for young adolescents (usually between ages 9 and 14). HPV is a very common virus that can lead to cervical cancer later in life, and the vaccine is safe and highly effective.

Why Testing Matters

Because many STIs are asymptomatic (meaning you feel perfectly fine), regular testing is the only way to know your status for sure. If left untreated, some STIs can lead to long-term health complications, including infertility.

Health centers and Youth Corners in Rwanda provide confidential and professional testing. It is normal and responsible to ask for an STI test if you are sexually active.

Open Communication

Talking about sexual health can feel awkward, but it is necessary. Discussing STI statuses, testing, and protection with a partner is a vital part of a healthy, safe relationship.
