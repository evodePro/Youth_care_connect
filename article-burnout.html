Understanding & Managing Burnout

4 Min Read

Adolescence is a crucial period for developing social and emotional habits important for mental well-being. However, the pressures of school, family expectations, and social environments can sometimes lead to chronic stress, commonly known as burnout.

"Globally, one in seven 10-19-year-olds experiences a mental disorder, accounting for 13% of the global burden of disease in this age group." – World Health Organization (WHO)

What is Burnout?

Burnout is a state of emotional, physical, and mental exhaustion caused by excessive and prolonged stress. It occurs when you feel overwhelmed, emotionally drained, and unable to meet constant demands.

Common Signs of Burnout:

  • Feeling tired or drained most of the time
  • Frequent headaches or muscle pain
  • Change in appetite or sleep habits
  • Sense of failure and self-doubt
  • Feeling helpless, trapped, or defeated
  • Detachment or feeling alone in the world
  • Loss of motivation
  • Skipping responsibilities or isolating yourself from others

WHO-Recommended Strategies for Mental Well-being

1. Emotional Regulation & Self-Care

Learning to manage emotions is a critical skill. Incorporate basic self-care into your daily routine. This includes prioritizing sleep (adolescents need 8-10 hours), eating nutritious meals, and engaging in regular physical activity. The WHO highlights that physical activity is protective against depression.

2. Building Supportive Environments

Your environment matters. Connect with friends and family members who make you feel safe and heard. Social support acts as a buffer against stress and helps you navigate difficult periods.

3. Knowing When to Seek Help

If burnout is affecting your ability to function daily—such as keeping you from attending school or participating in activities you used to enjoy—it is important to seek professional help. Early intervention is key. Half of all mental health conditions start by 14 years of age, but most cases are undetected and untreated.
